# Scoring Criteria — Matthew Pennisi

Matthew is a strategy/analytics leader with 10+ years, frequently the SOLE
analytical or product partner embedded in a team (not part of a large data
org). Real tools: SQL, dbt, Snowflake, BigQuery, Databricks, Looker, Fivetran.
No verified Python, statistics, or ML background. PM experience (Trusted
Health) plus BI/analytics engineering experience (Square, Snowflake, Hired,
Nomad, Promise).

Score every posting on these axes. Don't just pattern-match keywords —
actually reason about whether the role's *scope* fits, the way a good
recruiter would.

## 1. Seniority match (the axis that trips up job boards the most)
- **Red flag — underleveled**: JD asks for 1–3 or 4–7 years, or explicitly
  frames the role as someone's "first leadership role." Matthew would be
  over-qualified; comp and scope will likely undershoot his level even if the
  work looks familiar. (Real example: Parafin's 1–3 year Analytics Engineer
  req, Aspire's "first major leadership role" framing — both flagged as
  mismatches, not disqualifiers, but worth naming plainly.)
- **Good match**: 5–10+ years, or language like "own X from scratch," "sole
  analytical partner," "hands-on, building" combined with real scope
  (roadmap ownership, cross-functional partnership at the VP/founder level).
- Don't auto-reject underleveled roles — surface the mismatch honestly and
  let Matthew decide (e.g., equity upside, foot-in-the-door, intentional
  lifestyle reset).

## 2. Skill match — don't let a good vibe paper over a real gap
- Compare required/preferred tools against the verified list in
  `assets/resume_master.md`. SQL/dbt/Snowflake/BigQuery/Databricks/Looker/
  Fivetran = real. Python, R, statistics, A/B testing frameworks, ML
  (scikit-learn, TensorFlow), Airflow/Dagster, ClickHouse = NOT verified.
- If Python/stats/ML appear as *required* quals (not "nice to have"), that's
  a genuine capability gap, not a framing problem — say so plainly. (Real
  example: Intuitive's PM role required Python/R + A/B testing + ML frameworks
  as core, not peripheral — flagged as a real gap, not stretched around.)
- If those tools appear only under "preferred"/"bonus," it's a minor ding, not
  disqualifying.

## 3. Hands-on vs. strategic balance
- Matthew's strongest stories combine both: building the dashboards himself
  AND owning the roadmap/strategy layered on top (Trusted Health, Broccoli).
- Roles that are pure IC execution with no ownership, or pure strategy with no
  hands-on warehouse work, are weaker fits — call this out specifically.

## 4. Domain
- Real domain experience: healthcare marketplaces (Nomad, Trusted), fintech-
  adjacent (Square), gov/payments (Promise), enterprise SaaS/sales (Snowflake),
  recruiting marketplace (Hired).
- Domain mismatch is a minor factor, not a blocker — call it out briefly but
  don't weight it heavily unless the JD is domain-expertise-heavy (e.g. deep
  clinical, legal, or regulatory knowledge required).

## 5. Customer-facing analytics
- Matthew's clearest customer-facing (external users) proof point is Promise
  (enterprise utility customers). Trusted Health's platform was internal-
  facing (clinicians/finance/ops) — don't reframe it as customer-facing.
- If a JD emphasizes external/customer-facing reporting, lean on the Promise
  story and flag Trusted Health honestly as internal-facing.

## Output tiers
Use these labels when presenting results, consistent with prior evaluations
in this thread:
- **Strong fit** — seniority, skills, and scope all line up (e.g. Broccoli).
- **Worth a look, flag the gap** — good fit on most axes but one real
  mismatch worth surfacing before applying (seniority, one missing skill,
  domain).
- **Weak fit** — meaningful mismatch on seniority AND/OR skills; still show
  it if it's close, but say plainly why it's weaker than the others.

Never silently exclude a role that comes up in search — show it with an
honest score. Matthew decides what to pursue, not the skill.
